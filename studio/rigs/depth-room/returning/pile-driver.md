---
title: Pile Driver
hook: You can carry everything in the junkyard, stacked on your head, and you can only drop the top thing.
---
**Ten-second clip:** A tiny scavenger waddles across a scrapyard under a swaying 30-metre tower of fridges, bikes and a bathtub. A customer holds up a picture of a kettle. The kettle is 40 items down. The scavenger groans, starts handing junk off the top, and the tower leans into a crane.

**The rule:** Your inventory is infinite and physical: every pickup lands on top of your stack, and only the top item can ever come off.

**Loop:** Customers show pictogram orders. You plan pickups so the next order sits near the top, unstack onto temporary piles, then walk the wobbling tower under wires, through gates and past wind.

**Minute 100:** Orders chain and overlap, piles you leave become terrain, magnets and dogs interfere, and the yard gets reshuffled by a crusher. Expert play is a live stack-sorting algorithm under physics.

**Why watch:** The tower. Every streamer will refuse to drop the piano, and everyone will see the bridge coming.

**Hand use:** Sort is the stack-ordering puzzle; junkyard is the world and stock; no text means pictogram orders and zero UI words; the limitless inventory is the tower itself.

**Prototype question:** Is planning a physical last-in-first-out stack while balancing it fun, or just fiddly?

**Look:** Chunky low-poly rust, one bright orange stripe on the scavenger.

**Risk:** Balancing and planning fight each other; if wobble is too punishing, nobody gets to enjoy the puzzle.
