---
title: Roll Up the Distance
hook: The landscape is a carpet: roll it up to bring distant places together, then unroll your disastrous shortcuts.
---
**Ten-second clip:** A walker faces a canyon and distant house. They roll the ground into a fat cylinder; the house slides closer. The cylinder crushes a tree, then unrolls into a bridge over the canyon.
**The rule:** Ground can be rolled from either end; everything attached travels with it, while loose objects tumble off.
**Loop:** Roll a strip of terrain to compress a journey, carry the roll through a gap, and unroll it where its attached features solve the next crossing.
**Minute 100:** Branching strips, heavy buildings, rivers that spill and bridges stitched between carpets turn travel into moving whole pieces of geography.
**Why watch:** The destination gets wonderfully close just before its doorway rotates underneath the roll.
**Hand use:** Free pitch.
**Prototype question:** Does rolling and redeploying one terrain strip produce understandable physical surprises with a house, a tree, loose rocks and a gap?
**Look:** Felt diorama, thick turf backing, buildings pinned like model miniatures.
**Risk:** Deforming terrain could feel like an obscure editor; obvious roll handles and tactile rotation must make the action readable.
