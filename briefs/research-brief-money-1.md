# Research brief: AI income opportunities, run 1

## 1. Mission
Find nontraditional ways to make money online that a solo operator plus AI
agents can run: content and creator income, digital products, AI-assisted
services sold to others, newsletters, affiliate sites and similar. Produce a
ranked longlist, a screened top 5 and up to two shortlisted opportunities
with evidence, so I can pick one to test cheaply. Zero finalists is fine.

This team runs alongside the niche-discovery team (research-run-2), which
covers software and B2B products. Don't research those.

## 2. No personal interests
Ignore my interests. Don't favor any topic, platform or audience; follow the
evidence. At most 2 longlist opportunities per platform.

## 3. What makes an opportunity good
**Target:** $5,000/month profit within 12 months, starting under $1,000.

Score 1-5, multiply by weight. Maximum 55.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Verified earnings | 3 | Several independent operators show real revenue: platform payout screenshots with context, public revenue reports, marketplace sales counts. Not course sellers' claims |
| Durability | 3 | Platform rules clearly allow it, and it doesn't depend on a loophole, a trend or one algorithm change |
| Agent leverage | 2 | AI agents can do most of the ongoing work; my weekly time stays small |
| Room to enter | 2 | Demand is growing or the format isn't saturated; a newcomer can still stand out |
| Time to first dollar | 1 | First revenue within about 60 days |

Economics is a pass/fail check at the screen and scored in the deep dive.

## 4. Hard no's
An opportunity is out if it:
- Requires my face, voice or personal brand (faceless formats only).
- Breaks platform rules or depends on getting around them: undisclosed
  realistic AI content, mass-produced or "inauthentic" content farms, fake
  engagement, reuploading others' work.
- Involves deception: fake reviews or testimonials, impersonation, misleading
  income claims, undisclosed affiliate links, clickbait that misrepresents.
- Uses copyrighted material we don't have rights to.
- Is gambling, trading signals, crypto promotion, adult content, or health,
  legal or financial advice.
- Needs more than $1,000 upfront, physical inventory, or a license over $100.
- Mostly makes money by selling "how to make money" courses.

## 5. Evidence rules
Same as research-run-2 section 5, plus:
- **Income claims need proof.** Count only earnings shown with context
  (platform dashboards, public reports, marketplace data). Discount anything
  from people selling courses, coaching or tools about that same method.
- **Check the platform's current rules** for every opportunity: monetization
  eligibility, AI-content disclosure, and policies on repetitive or
  mass-produced content. Link the policy page you read.
- Web access follows briefs/RESEARCH-WEB-ACCESS-RULE.md (v2): built-in tools first, then
  official access, Exa, and a logged-out browser at human pace for verdict-deciding pages.
  Never log in, solve CAPTCHAs, rotate proxies or bulk-scrape. Log every fallback page.
- Note survivorship: for every success story, look for evidence of how many
  people tried and failed.

## 6. How the team works
Three seats. Heavy reading on Codex; only the skeptic uses Claude.
- **Lead (Codex):** owns the funnel, scores, writes the notes, keeps
  research/money-1/niches.json current.
- **Scout (Codex):** finds opportunities and their evidence: creator
  economy reports, marketplace data (Etsy, Gumroad, Whop, Payhip), public
  revenue reports, platform policy pages, forums where operators compare
  results.
- **Skeptic (Claude):** tries to kill each screened opportunity: platform
  policy risk, saturation, hidden costs, survivorship bias, and whether the
  evidence is really from course sellers. Cites opened pages only, and states
  what evidence would overturn each objection.

Funnel:
1. **Longlist:** 15 opportunities, at most 2 per platform, each with one line
   and at least one link.
2. **Screen to 5:** each needs verified earnings from at least 3 independent
   operators, a policy page showing it's allowed, and an Economics pass.
3. **Skeptic pass on the 5**, one line each.
4. **Checkpoint 1:** report and wait.
5. **Deep dive** the ones I choose, with the same verdict categories as
   research-run-2: contradicted, insufficient evidence, worth a small test,
   shortlist. The lead answers each skeptic objection before closing one.
6. **Checkpoint 2:** report and wait.

## 7. Deliverables

## 8. Operations and limits
Same as research-run-2 sections 8 and 9: fresh sessions, permissions set and
dry-run tested before dispatch, per-seat file prefixes, no spending, no
accounts, no posting, no contacting anyone. Start about 30 minutes after
research-run-2 so the two teams don't hit checkpoints together. If usage
warnings appear, this team pauses first.
